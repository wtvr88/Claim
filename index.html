Who Is Eligible?

Many car buyers were sold hire purchase (HP) or personal contract purchase (PCP) finance deals between April 2007 and 28 January 2021. If you took out such a loan and the dealer did not clearly disclose the commission they earned, or if they arranged a higher interest rate to earn more commission under a discretionary commission arrangement (DCA), you may have been mis‑sold[1]. Other examples of mis‑selling include pressure selling without sufficient affordability checks, misleading information, or hidden fees and penalties[2]. Even if your finance has ended, you could still claim compensation.

  • You purchased a car using PCP or HP finance between 2007 and January 2021.
  • The finance was for personal use (not a business lease).
  • A commission arrangement existed and was not clearly explained to you[3].
  • You paid a higher interest rate due to hidden commission or were not offered the best available rate.
  • You felt pressured into the agreement or were not given time to consider alternatives.

Unsure if you qualify? Call us on 123 456 789 or fill out our contact form below. We’ll assess your case for free.

How Much Could You Receive?

The Financial Conduct Authority estimates that consumers could receive around £950 for each mis‑sold car finance agreement[4]. Other legal experts suggest typical payouts average about £1,100 per claim and note that many people have two or three agreements[5]. That means individuals could be owed more than £4,000[6]! The exact amount depends on factors such as the size of your loan, length of the contract and how much extra interest you paid due to hidden commission.

Our team will calculate the difference between the interest you actually paid and what you should have paid. If we succeed, you get the money back. If we don’t win, you owe us nothing.
